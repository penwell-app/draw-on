Draw-On animates **any inline SVG** so that it draws itself: the strokes come on
first, then the fills and the labels fade in behind them. It is the same engine
that [Penwell](https://usepenwell.com/) uses to animate Mermaid diagrams and
handwritten notebook pages, packaged as a small, framework-agnostic library.

`import { createDrawing } from '@penwell/draw-on/core'` — that is the whole idea.
Give it an `<svg>` element, call `play()`, and the drawing appears.

---

## The problem it solves

SVG animation libraries such as [Vivus](https://github.com/maxwellito/vivus) are
very good at one thing: animating the outline of a shape by walking it like a
pen stroke. That works beautifully for line art.

Real diagrams rarely look like line art. Mermaid, Excalidraw, Figma exports and
most design tools produce markup where:

- boxes and nodes are **filled shapes** with `fill` set and no `stroke` at all,
- arrows and connectors are thin strokes that use markers,
- labels are `<text>` elements that sit on top of everything,
- decorations use filters, gradients, dashes and opacity.

If you hand that markup to a stroke-only animator, the filled boxes have nothing
to draw, and the text is on screen before the drawing starts. The result is a
diagram that pops into existence rather than drawing itself.

## The mental model

Draw-On keeps the stroke animation and adds the two missing beats around it:

| Beat | What happens | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Strokes** | Every shape is animated as an outline, one by one | Stroke-only shapes already look right |
| **2. Fills** | Filled shapes are given a temporary outline so they can participate | A filled box with no stroke has no length to draw |
| **3. Labels** | `<text>`, `<image>` and `<foreignObject>` stay hidden, then fade in | Text on screen too early breaks the illusion |

Then the temporary outlines disappear and the fills fade in, so the finished
frame is exactly your original artwork.

## What you get

- **Progressive strokes** with three ordering strategies: `oneByOne`, `delayed` and `sync`.
- **Fill synthesis** — fill-only shapes are outlined, drawn, then filled.
- **A reveal window** controlled by `revealStart` and `revealMs`.
- **Scrubbing** — `drawing.setProgress(0.5)` puts the animation anywhere you like, in both directions.
- **In-browser recording** — `recordDrawToWebM` renders every frame to a WebM clip. No server, no ffmpeg.
- **React bindings** — `useSketchDraw` and the unstyled `SketchDraw` component.
- **ESM and CJS builds**, tree-shakeable, with TypeScript types included.

## When it is not

- You want morphing, physics or keyframe timelines across many SVGs — this is a
  draw-on effect, not a general animation engine.
- Your artwork must stay untouched in the DOM and you cannot pass a clone — the
  engine mutates the element it is given (it says so again in
  [How it works](/docs/how-it-works)).
- You need MP4 output. `MediaRecorder` MP4 support is inconsistent across
  browsers, so Draw-On ships WebM only; see
  [Recording](/docs/guides/recording).
